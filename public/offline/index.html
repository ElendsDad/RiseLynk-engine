Offline capability

What works with no signal, and what does not.

ASME A17.1 Section 8.6 wants the records on site. On site is a machine room, a pit, or a basement, and that is exactly where the signal is not. So the field app works without one. Here is the whole boundary, including the parts that are awkward for us.

STAGED, not published. This page is not linked, not in the sitemap, and marked noindex until it clears founder review.

Why we publish the limits

Every vendor in this trade says their app works offline. None of them says what it does not do. That is not a small omission, because a mechanic finds out the answer standing in a pit at four in the afternoon, and by then the sale is somebody else's problem.

So this is the whole matrix. If a row here is awkward for us, it stayed in. Read it before you buy anything, including ours.

The entrapment row, said plainly

A person in a car is a life-safety call and it does not wait for software. Raise it on the phone if that is what you have, and it is recorded properly. Then get to signal, or call dispatch. No maintenance app should be the only path to a trapped passenger, ours included.

What happens to the work itself

Offline is easy to claim. What separates a real offline app from a checkbox is what happens on the way back in. Four answers most vendors do not give.

Two people edited the same ticket. Now what?

The merge runs field by field, not whole record over whole record. If you changed the notes and the office changed the billing flag, both changes survive. Only when you and the office change the same field does the office version stand, and you are shown what was replaced, with your value next to it, so you can put it back if it mattered.

So no, your edits are not all guaranteed to survive. Some are replaced, on purpose, and you get told which. A vendor who promises nothing is ever lost has either not thought about it or is not telling you.

The server refused one of my writes.

It gets parked somewhere you can see it, with a retry button and a discard button. It is never dropped quietly. That path has its own regression test, because it once had a bug that could lose a row on a failed retry, and we do not want it back.

How much history sits on the phone, and for how long?

There is no date window and no expiry. The phone keeps pulling until it has the whole record it is allowed to see, and it keeps it. The practical limit is the phone's own storage, not a rolling cache.

Phones do clear storage for apps they think are abandoned. RiseLynk asks the phone to keep its data, and warns the technician when the phone will not promise to. That is a best effort, not a guarantee, and we would rather say so than let a week-old day disappear quietly.

The phone went down the hoistway before it synced.

Work that never reached the server is gone with the phone. There is no copy of an unsent edit anywhere else, because there is nowhere else for it to be. Wiping a phone is blocked while anything is still waiting to sync, which covers the accident, not the drop.

This is a physical limit, not a software gap, and every offline app on earth has it. It is on this page because you should hear it from us rather than find out.
